# Lab Meeting - August 13, 2026

Today's papers all sit inside the same problem: how a drone should fly to capture a city well enough to rebuild it in 3D. UrbanScene3D is the dataset the rest of this line leans on, giving real and synthetic scenes with ground truth to actually measure reconstruction quality. The TOG 2022 paper and its 2025 ISPRS follow-up both try to predict that quality during planning instead of using hand-crafted rules, and the newer one fixes a real weakness in the older one by predicting accuracy and completeness jointly instead of separately. FUEL is the outlier here, it's about exploring fast rather than reconstructing well, but it's a useful contrast in how the planning problem changes once the goal changes.

## Capturing, Reconstructing, and Simulating: the UrbanScene3D Dataset

Liqiang Lin, Yilin Liu, Yue Hu, Xingguang Yan, Ke Xie, Hui Huang. ECCV 2022. https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04286

Outdoor 3D vision has lagged behind indoor work because good outdoor data is hard to get: ground based sets like KITTI and Cityscapes have narrow fields of view, drone sets built for perception only capture partial views, and synthetic sets like HoliCity are clean but lack real texture. UrbanScene3D fixes this by pairing 16 synthetic and real drone captured urban scenes, totaling 136 km² and over 128,000 images, with the actual flight paths, geometric proxies at four fidelity levels, and ground truth used to reconstruct them, all wrapped in an Unreal Engine and AirSim simulator. Real scenes were captured with DJI rigs and reconstructed with the commercial tool ContextCapture, then scored against Trimble X7 LiDAR ground truth accurate to roughly 2 mm. Four aerial path planners were run on the same scenes: oblique photography was cheapest on energy but gave the roughest reconstructions, while the two learned and optimized planners clearly won on accuracy and completeness. The dataset ends up larger than KITTI, Cityscapes, HoliCity, and similar benchmarks on most axes. Main gap worth flagging: reconstruction depends on a closed-source commercial tool, so results aren't fully reproducible with open pipelines like COLMAP.


## Learning Reconstructability for Drone Aerial Path Planning

Yilin Liu, Liqiang Lin, Yue Hu, Ke Xie, Chi-Wing Fu, Hao Zhang, Hui Huang. ACM Transactions on Graphics 41, 2022 (SIGGRAPH Asia). https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10174

Aerial path planners need to estimate, during planning, how well a scene will reconstruct from a set of viewpoints, but the ground truth doesn't exist yet. Prior planners lean on hand crafted reconstructability heuristics that aren't validated against real reconstruction error and degrade badly when the planning proxy is inaccurate. This paper replaces the heuristic with a learned predictor: two attention based networks, trained on simulated reconstructions from UrbanScene3D, first score reconstructability from geometry alone and then refine that score using images already captured mid-flight to account for proxy uncertainty. The predictor drops into existing planners, and the authors also build a new iterative planner around it with adaptive point sampling and view selection. Against the standard Smith et al. heuristic, correlation with true reconstruction quality improves roughly 34% without images and around 150% when image-conditioned on uncertain proxies, and the new planner reaches noticeably higher F-scores on real scenes than the prior best baseline. The authors flag real limitations: it ignores surface material effects, proxy matching breaks down for large gaps, and computation is quadratic in viewpoint count. Worth asking how much of that correlation gain translates into fewer flight passes in practice.


## FUEL: Fast UAV Exploration using Incremental Frontier Structure and Hierarchical Planning

Boyu Zhou, Yichen Zhang, Xinyi Chen, Shaojie Shen. IEEE RA-L / ICRA 2021. https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11561

Autonomous drone exploration tends to be slower than it needs to be: planners greedily chase the nearest frontier or point of maximum information gain, fly conservatively, and carry enough computational overhead that fast replanning isn't practical. FUEL fixes this with an incrementally updated frontier information structure that only rechecks clusters whose bounding boxes intersect a changed map region, avoiding a full rescan every cycle, paired with a three-stage planner: a global tour over the whole space solved as an asymmetric TSP, local viewpoint refinement nearby, and a minimum-time B-spline trajectory that jointly optimizes control points and timing under safety and dynamics constraints. In a bridge scenario, average exploration time dropped from 575-857 seconds for baseline planners to 104 seconds, a 3-8x speedup with much lower variance, and similar gains held in a large maze. It was also flown fully onboard on real hardware in indoor and outdoor tests. The incremental frontier structure is the real contribution: it's what makes high-frequency replanning possible at all. The paper doesn't model pose drift affecting the map, and tests stay in small to medium bounded spaces, so scalability to much larger or multi-agent settings is untested.


## Aerial Path Planning for 3D Urban Scene Reconstruction with Dual-Task Reconstructability Learning and Adaptive Viewpoint Selection

Tianrui Shen, Yingmei Wei, Lai Kang, Shanshan Wan, Haixuan Wang, Yee-Hong Yang. ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing, vol. 227, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2025.05.015

This is a direct follow-on to the TOG 2022 reconstructability paper above, from a different group. That earlier line trains reconstruction accuracy and completeness as two separate prediction phases; this paper argues the two signals are correlated aspects of the same underlying reconstructability and should be predicted jointly. A single network with soft parameter sharing predicts both at once, with the two loss terms weighted by learnable parameters instead of hand-tuned ones, and the resulting scores drive an adaptive step that repositions and prunes an initial viewpoint set before it's connected into a flight path. Reported results: about 3.6% higher F-score than prior planning methods while using 15% fewer images, and 6% higher than oblique photography with 40% less image data.
