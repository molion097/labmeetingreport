## Learning an Invariant Hilbert Space for Domain Adaptation

Samitha Herath, Mehrtash Harandi, and Fatih Porikli. CVPR 2017. https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08350

Most domain adaptation methods pick a subspace first, usually via PCA on one domain, and only afterward work out how to transfer knowledge into it, which the authors call an arbitrary, unjustified split. This paper learns the space and the transfer jointly instead. Source and target samples are each mapped by their own projection into a shared latent Hilbert space, with a Mahalanobis metric learned in that space at the same time, combining a discriminative large-margin loss over labeled pairs with an unsupervised term that matches the means and covariances of the projected source and target distributions via the Stein divergence between covariance matrices. Because the projections must stay orthogonal and the metric must stay positive definite, the whole thing is optimized as one problem over the product of the corresponding Riemannian manifolds rather than plain projected gradient descent, which the authors found unstable in practice. Classification at test time is just nearest neighbor in the learned space. On the Office+Caltech10 benchmark across Amazon, Webcam, DSLR and Caltech, their method gets the best score on most of the twelve domain pairs for both SURF and deep VGG-FC6 features, for example 95.5 percent versus 90.7 percent for the prior best on one pairing, and it also improves as pose difference grows on the PIE multiview face dataset. It's a clean example of folding representation learning and distribution alignment into one geometric optimization instead of two disjoint steps, though the Riemannian machinery adds real optimization overhead compared to simpler alignment methods.
