## Semantic Hilbert Space for Text Representation Learning

Benyou Wang, Qiuchi Li, Massimo Melucci, Dawei Song. The World Wide Web Conference (WWW) 2019. https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313516

The starting complaint here is that most text models compose word vectors linearly, but a lot of meaning is not linear at all, "ivory tower" is not just "ivory" plus "tower". The authors borrow the formalism of quantum probability theory to build a Semantic Hilbert Space, a single complex-valued vector space where sememes, words, sentences and a new learned abstraction all coexist. Each word is a unit-length complex vector over a basis of sememes (minimal meaning units), treated like a quantum particle in a superposition state, with amplitude and phase encoding different layers of meaning such as co-occurrence or sentiment. A sentence, taken as a bag of words, is represented not by summing but as a density matrix, a weighted mixture of the words' superposition states, whose complex off-diagonal entries capture interference between words, exactly where the non-linear composition comes from. A set of trainable measurement projectors is applied to this density matrix via the Born rule to pull out task-relevant probabilities feeding a final classifier, wired together end to end as what the authors call the Quantum Probability Driven Network. On six standard text classification benchmarks covering sentiment, subjectivity and question type, the network matches or edges out CNN and BiLSTM baselines while staying more interpretable, since each layer corresponds to a specific quantum-probability operation rather than an opaque kernel or gate. It's quantum-inspired math, not quantum computing, and the interpretability story is more compelling than the accuracy gains, which are real but modest against strong neural baselines.
