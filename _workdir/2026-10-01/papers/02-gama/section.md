## GAMA: A Large Audio-Language Model with Advanced Audio Understanding and Complex Reasoning Abilities

Ghosh et al., EMNLP 2024. https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.361/ (arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11768)

Earlier large audio-language models such as LTU and SALMONN bolt an audio encoder onto an LLM through a simple linear connector, which limits alignment and does poorly on questions that need multi-step reasoning about a sound scene. GAMA feeds the LLM three kinds of audio input at once: a custom Audio Q-Former for semantic content, AST features with a multi-layer aggregator that pools low-level and high-level layers, and AST event tags given as a text soft prompt. It is first fine-tuned on more than 2.5M audio-caption pairs, with LLM-written caption rewrites for augmentation, following the LTU recipe with only LoRA trained on the LLM. It is then instruction-tuned on CompA-R, a GPT-generated set of about 200K reasoning question-answer pairs built from audio metadata, plus a 1,561-item human-verified test set. GAMA beats the baselines on classification and captioning by 1% to 84%, and GAMA-IT leads on open-ended and reasoning questions by 8% to 58% on CompA-R-test, judged by GPT-4 and humans. The Q-Former ablation hurts most. The reasoning data is synthetic and GPT-judged, so scores should be read with caution, but the connector design is a useful template.
