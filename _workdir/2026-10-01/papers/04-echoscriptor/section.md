## EchoScriptor: Automatic Lifelogging Narratives via Activity-Based Audio-Language Model

Kaylee Yaxuan Li, Xinghao Zhou, Haizhong Zheng, Kang G. Shin, and Alanson P. Sample. CHI 2026. https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791528

Camera-free lifelogging is attractive for memory rehabilitation, personal informatics and assistive use, but most audio-based systems stop at isolated event labels, which gives little context and no narrative thread across a day. This paper pairs an activity-based audio-language model with language reasoning so that raw in-home audio becomes a coherent written account of what happened and what the acoustic surroundings were like. EchoScriptor is described as an end-to-end pipeline that goes from recorded sound to context-aware natural-language descriptions and then to a narrative lifelog. I could not get the full text, since the publisher page blocked automated download, so this write-up rests on the abstract and I cannot say much about the architecture or training data. At the moment level it reports 94.15% activity recognition accuracy and 89.25% background recognition accuracy. At the summary level it reaches an F1 of 0.92 and beats a baseline that runs a sound classifier followed by an LLM. In a user study with 20 participants watching 10 household activity videos, its summaries were rated highly and came close to the perceived usefulness of human-written ones. My take is that moving from event detection to narrative is the right direction, though I would want to see results on long real recordings and a discussion of privacy for always-on home audio.
