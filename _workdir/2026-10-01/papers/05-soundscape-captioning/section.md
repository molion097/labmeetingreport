## Soundscape Captioning Using Sound Affective Quality Network and Large Language Model

Yuanbo Hou, Qiaoqiao Ren, Andrew Mitchell, Wenwu Wang, Jian Kang, Tony Belpaeme, Dick Botteldooren. IEEE Transactions on Multimedia 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05914

Audio scene and event classifiers say what is in a recording but not how it feels to a listener, which is the point of soundscape research. The authors define an affective soundscape captioning task and build SoundSCaper for it. Its acoustic model, SoundAQnet, is a small multi-scale CNN with a graph fusion layer (2.7M parameters) that jointly predicts the acoustic scene, 15 audio event classes and the eight perceived affective qualities, trained on ARAUS, roughly 25,000 clips rated by 605 participants. These outputs go to an off-the-shelf GPT-3.5-Turbo through prompt engineering and token management rather than fine-tuning, which is meant to curb hallucination. SoundAQnet reaches 95.31% scene accuracy on the test set. Two juries of 16 experts and 16 laypersons rated captions on 60 clips. Experts scored SoundSCaper slightly below two soundscape experts, not significantly, and laypersons found them comparable (3.24 versus 3.20 on the external set). It also beats audio captioning baselines such as GAMA and Qwen-Audio, for example CIDEr 0.2745 against 0.1213. The label set is small, so it misses sounds like sirens, and cultural subjectivity stays open. Still, a lightweight acoustic model feeding a generic LLM is a practical design.
