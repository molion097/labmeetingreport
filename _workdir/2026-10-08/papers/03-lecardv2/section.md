## LeCaRDv2: A Large-Scale Chinese Legal Case Retrieval Dataset

Haitao Li, Yunqiu Shao, Yueyue Wu, Qingyao Ai, Yixiao Ma, Yiqun Liu. SIGIR 2024 (arXiv preprint 2023). https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17609

Chinese legal case retrieval has been held back by small datasets, relevance labels that only look at fact similarity, and candidate pools built from a few lexical models. LeCaRDv1 had just 107 queries and 10,700 candidates over 20 charges. LeCaRDv2 draws 800 queries and 55,192 annotated candidates from about 4.3 million criminal judgments, covering 50 charges. Queries are balanced by charge and by difficulty, using entropy of a judgment-prediction model, plus 100 controversial and 100 procedural queries. Candidates come from a two-level pool. First, BM25, a dense legal RoBERTa and a law-article similarity score (inverse provision frequency) each give 100 cases. Then runs from CAIL2021 narrow these to about 30 promising cases. Legal experts label overall relevance plus characterization, penalty and procedure relevance, following Supreme People's Court guidance. Baselines are evaluated with recall on the full corpus. Lexical models are strong, reaching roughly 0.72 to 0.74 recall@1000 zero-shot, while generic Chinese BERT variants are far worse. Fine-tuned retrieval-oriented models (SEED, RetroMAE) reach about 0.81 to 0.82 at R@1000, yet BM25 still wins at R@100. My take is that this is a useful, carefully built benchmark, though annotation agreement and the heavy criminal-only scope deserve a question or two.
