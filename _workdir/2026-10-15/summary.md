# Lab Meeting - October 15, 2026

This set is about using LLMs to simulate people in order to study teaching. Generative Agents is the foundation, an architecture for believable simulated people. Classroom Simulacra and AgentSchool apply it to students and whole schools, one grounded in real course data, the other in structured knowledge and misconception states. EduClaw-Bench turns simulated learners into a testbed for judging tutor agents over weeks. The learner-modelling paper is the sobering counterpoint, showing an LLM alone is a worse model of what a student knows than a small knowledge-tracing network.

## Problems With Large Language Models for Learner Modelling: Why LLMs Alone Fall Short for Responsible Tutoring in K-12 Education

Danial Hooshyar, Yeongwook Yang, Gustav Sir, Tommi Kärkkäinen, Raija Hämäläinen, Mutlu Cukurova, Roger Azevedo. arXiv preprint (cs.AI) 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23036

The authors argue that the spread of LLM tutors in schools has fostered a mistaken belief that general models can replace explicit learner modelling, which matters in K-12 because the EU AI Act treats education as high risk. Their test is deliberately narrow: can an LLM give accurate and stable real-time estimates of what a student knows? They compare a GRU-based Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT) model with Llama 3 8B, zero-shot and LoRA fine-tuned, on ASSISTments 2009-2010 (about 450k interactions, 7,981 students, 149 skills). The LLM outputs only 0 or 1, with probabilities taken from logits. DKT reaches AUC 0.83, the fine-tuned LLM 0.77 and the zero-shot one 0.69. DKT also has the lowest volatility (0.108) and the lowest rate of mastery updates that move against the student's answer (0.406, versus 0.501 zero-shot and 0.453 fine-tuned). Fine-tuning took about 198 hours on dual H100s, while DKT trained in under a minute on a T4. For one student, DKT made 9 inconsistent updates against 19 for the LLM. The case for hybrids, with KT supplying mastery and the LLM writing feedback, is convincing, though one model, one dataset and LoRA only limit generalization.

## Classroom Simulacra: Building Contextual Student Generative Agents in Online Education for Learning Behavioral Simulation

Songlin Xu, Hao-Ning Wen, Hongyi Pan, Dallas Dominguez, Dongyin Hu, and Xinyu Zhang. CHI 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02780

Student simulators are meant to act as a sandbox for teachers, but most ignore what was actually taught, because datasets rarely annotate course materials and LLMs struggle with long lecture text. The authors first ran a 6-week online workshop with 60 students and 12 hour-long lectures, logging post-test answers mapped to annotated slides. They then propose transferable iterative reflection (TIR). On training students, a reflective agent sees the true outcomes and explains its wrong predictions, and a novice agent without the labels must improve using that reflection. The loop repeats, switching direction when accuracy drops, and the best reflection is stored. At test time, reflections from four training students in the same course serve as demonstrations, and the same reflections also compress long inputs for a fine-tuned BERT. With GPT-4o-mini on their data, TIR lifts accuracy from 0.678 to 0.706 (standard prompting), 0.682 to 0.713 (chain of thought), and 0.651 to 0.703 (BERT), above the best knowledge-tracing baseline, SimpleKT, at 0.656. The simulated students also track real ones better across lectures, questions, and student pairs. A useful idea, though text-only slides and a single course limit generality.

## AgentSchool: An LLM-Powered Multi-Agent Simulation for Education

Yulei Ye, Wenhao Li, Zhong Wen, et al. arXiv preprint (cs.AI), May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30144

Trials of educational AI on real learners are slow and hard to undo, and many LLM classroom simulators only role-play personas. AgentSchool instead treats learning as state transition. Student agents carry weighted subject knowledge graphs, pools of thinking workflows and explicit misconceptions, while teacher agents plan, scaffold and reflect along the Zone of Proximal Development. A scenery generator sets up formal and informal settings, and the simulator separates interaction scale, time granularity and duration. In a 2 by 3 lesson study over five backbone LLMs, the structured students beat the baseline simulator on average mastery (about 0.31 against 0.13 on Claude-sonnet-4) and showed richer misconception traces (12 against 2), though more misconceptions are not automatically better. The teacher comparison is mixed: the adaptive teacher led on only some backbones, and a scripted teacher won on others. In 300-round recess scenes with 11 and 30 agents, they report peripheral introverts, cliques, hubs and aggressor-induced cohesion among the rest. I like the explicit state tracking, but the evidence is preliminary and uncalibrated against real classrooms.

## EduClaw-Bench: A Long-Horizon Benchmark for Pedagogical LLM Agents with Simulated Learners

Unggi Lee, Sookbun Lee, Yeil Jeong, Eunjoo Lee, Minchul Shin, Hoilym Kwon. arXiv preprint (cs.CY) 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03206

Most tutor benchmarks grade one turn or session against fixed student lines, so they cannot tell whether a student actually learns over weeks. Here an agent tutor works through a learning management system for 30 virtual days with a simulated learner whose answers are driven by a knowledge tracing model trained on real student data (XES3G5M) and whose mastery is probed daily for learning gain. Eleven personalities crossed with five study schedules give 55 scenarios. Tutors are scored on learning gain, responsiveness, and helpfulness, plus two curriculum axes (Gagne and Rosenshine) judged by a three-model panel. Ten agent frameworks on three base models show that no framework leads on more than one tier, so single-tier leaderboards mislead, and every agent's learning plateaus within 5 to 10 days. The best curriculum scores are about 1.9 out of 5. Learner calibration error is 0.049, and helpfulness on 150 real transcripts from about 500 students (6.02) matches simulation. Judge agreement with human experts is only moderate on helpfulness (0.33). Good framing for long-horizon evaluation, but the judges are the weak link.

## Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior

Joon Sung Park, Joseph C. O'Brien, Carrie J. Cai, Meredith Ringel Morris, Percy Liang, Michael S. Bernstein. UIST 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442

Earlier LLM agents could produce a plausible line of dialogue but not a coherent life over time. This paper wraps ChatGPT in an architecture with a memory stream, a natural-language log of experiences retrieved by recency, importance, and relevance, plus reflection that condenses memories into higher-level inferences, and planning that breaks a daily agenda into finer actions and revises it when events occur. Twenty-five agents populate Smallville, a Sims-like sandbox. In a study, 100 Prolific raters ranked interview answers from the full system, three ablations, and a crowdworker baseline. The full system scored highest (TrueSkill 29.89), ahead of no reflection (26.88), no reflection or planning (25.64), crowdworkers (22.95), and a memoryless baseline (21.21), an effect size of about eight standard deviations, and only crowdworkers versus that baseline were not significantly different. Over two simulated days, knowledge of a party spread from one agent to thirteen, network density rose from 0.167 to 0.74, and five of twelve invitees attended. Agents sometimes missed memories or embellished them. It is the base architecture that the education simulation papers here build on.
